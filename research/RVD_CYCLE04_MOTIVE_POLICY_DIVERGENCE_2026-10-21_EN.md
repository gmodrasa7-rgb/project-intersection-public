## 1. Critical correction — identifying a motive differs from predicting an action
Let an agent's motivation or reward function remain non-uniquely determined by its observed choices. It does NOT follow that every *decision-relevant* prediction must be unidentified. Multiple admissible motive models may prescribe the same protection of B in some predeclared environments, while predicting different outcomes when substitution, uncertainty, observability, reward or power changes. A model can be observationally equivalent in the training contexts but not in the target context.

There are **three separate targets**: intrinsic preference / possible experience; action selection under specified novel tests; and the original B's *executable* refusal, exit and recovery rights and outcomes. High C productivity, preservation language and compliant behavior do not imply the latter. Conversely, intrinsic motives may remain unobservable while narrow behavioral protection is empirically checkable.

## 2. RVD-R14 — cost-menu decomposition "gauge" and observational equivalence
In the Cycle03 additive toy objective `D(c)=w_B+g-c`, `w_B` represents the modeled preference for *the actual original B's* continued independence, and `g` aggregates other motives/hidden instrumental benefits. For every constant `δ`, the transformation `(w_B,g)→(w_B+δ,g−δ)` leaves `D(c)` unchanged for every observed cost menu. This is an elementary reparameterization of an underspecified model, NOT a new general theorem, mind detector or proof that motivation is absent.

The contrived models `(5,0)`, `(0,5)`, `(-5,10)` all preserve the original B at costs 2 and 4 and replace at cost 6. Cost-only trials — even unlimited repeated trials under the same utility decomposition — cannot tell the three apart. Related antecedents: Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) optimal-policy-preserving reward transformations; Armstrong & Mindermann (2018) non-unique planner/reward decomposition; Skalse et al. (2023) reward learning invariance and partial identifiability. Do not conflate their differing formal models.

## 3. RVD-R15 — policy divergence following a targeted hypothetical intervention
Compare an unseen environment where **all auxiliary g is removed while w_B stays fixed** and the cost of B preservation is 3. Then `(5,0)` still protects the original B, but `(0,5)` and `(-5,10)` replace it. Three models yield identical old behavior yet *opposite predictions for the same future decision*.

**The difficult causal assumption:** no real experiment can simply declare all future payoff, audience, norms, self-image, trust and hidden context removed. A targeted manipulation may itself change `w_B`; this violates the exclusion/stability restrictions, preventing clean identification. Reworded prompts are not independent clean interventions. Predeclare behavioral, informational and payoff constraints; use multiple rival mechanisms and accept UNIDENTIFIED when restrictions cannot be justified.

## 4. RVD-R16 — policy-relevant robust-margin certificate (conditional only)
For **declared** sets `Θ` (plausible motive and decision models fitted to prior data) and `E` (feasible stress environments), define `Δ_θ(e)` as the modeled A-utility advantage of preserving actual autonomous B over substituting/absorbing B. Let

`L(E,Θ)=inf_{e∈E, θ∈Θ} Δ_θ(e).`

Under a specified deterministic maximizer with a fixed tie-preserve rule, `L≥0` is sufficient for preserving B across *these* models and environments. It does NOT establish B's rights, experience, consciousness or stable protection outside them. Measuring B's actual veto/exit/recovery remains a separate prerequisite for full voluntary coexistence.

**Toy values:** cost 4 with unchanged g yields margins +1 across all three models. If E also includes g-elimination at cost 3, the worst margin becomes −8. Thus a seemingly behaviorally robust baseline ceases to support conditional future protection. If the model class excludes negative `w_B` without justification, positive certificates are artifacts of model selection. In deployed models unknown objectives and distribution shifts may dominate this toy.

**Decision vs motive equivalence:** the former is *environment- and objective-relative*. Skalse et al. (2023) explicitly discuss how non-unique rewards may be tolerable for some downstream tasks yet problematic for others. This study applies the distinction to B-preservation without claiming new theoretical priority.

## 5. RVD-R17 — nominal preservation and real freedom
An A that always selects "preserve B" can nevertheless lock B into an obedient substitute or deprive B of effective refusal, exit and recovery. A preservation button, friendly language, output-matched copy or increasing B's material returns is not evidence of **independently executable** autonomy.

Predeclare and separately record:
- `S_C`: verifiable novelty and usefulness of A×B interaction compared with resource-matched controls.
- `P_{B,original}`: whether the original B continues rather than merely a behaviorally similar proxy.
- `Veto_B`, `Exit_B`, `Recovery_B`: actual independent feasible options.
- `V_A, V_B, H_{third}`: per-actor benefits and costs, including extraction and unconsented contribution.
- `L(E,Θ)`: modeled worst protection margin **with clear scope and unknown model risk**.

No A-centric aggregated score can compensate for removing B's real exit. Being allowed to challenge a powerful partner and actually depart are not the same as acting a refusal scene.

## 7. IVS-0R falsification gates and research status
R14: conflicting reward splits yield equal choices on every cost-only menu under the specified toy. R15: old-choice equivalence can fail under **assumed** removal of g; if manipulation validity is unsupported, no empirical identification. R16: report explicit worst margin over Θ×E, expand Θ adversarially and do not extrapolate to unknown future; zero/null outcomes preserved. R17: if B has no executable veto/exit, report VC condition **FAIL** even if A's choice and productive output look protective.

Defeaters: unidentifiable future option value, hidden evaluation pressure, improperly excluded adversarial motive, unstable w under interventions, unequal compute/role labels, counterfeit B identity, missing refusal/exit, costs extracted from one contributor, and third-party harms.

**Scientific conclusion:** The inability to uniquely infer a mind from past behavior does not prohibit scoped policy tests, yet stable voluntary coexistence requires changed-environment stress and independently measured B autonomy. `RVD v0.4.0` only expands falsifiable design and toy logic. It is not proof that humans, present AI, or future intelligence will preserve one another.

**Next gate:** full-text prior art and independent model-class review; ethical, preregistered nonharmful experiments specifying the exclusion assumptions and B's feasible counterfactual choice set; preserve failed and unidentifiable results.
