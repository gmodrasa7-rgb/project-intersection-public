## 0. 핵심 발견: 타 개체 존속을 선택하는 내재적 선호는 *가능*하지만, 관측된 친사회적 행동으로 *식별되지 않음*
A stronger actor choosing to preserve B can be explained by at least six distinct, confusable latent channels:
1. Intrinsic valuation of B's wellbeing/continued existence (other-regarding utility);
2. inequity-aversion or preference for equitable distributions (which may ignore B's very existence);
3. norm compliance (contextual prescription, possibly altered by an authority);
4. private self-image or public reputation (influenced by audience and observability);
5. a desire to avoid information, obligation or blame ('moral wiggle room');
6. long-term insurance, reciprocal benefits and unknown future options (instrumental).

**This cycle found primary research on (1)–(5), not a proof of unconditional (1).** It rejects the false dichotomy 'all kindness is reputation' vs 'all kindness is unconditional stable benevolence'. Both have empirical support in narrow conditions; the mapping between behavior and latent motive remains underidentified.

### 0.1 Research result most threatening to naive VC measurement
[Dana, Cain & Dawes (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2005.10.001) observed that roughly a third of subjects in a dictator game gave up one dollar of their own payoff to quietly avoid making a $10 allocation decision; the counterpart did not learn the opportunity existed. **Agents sometimes pay to avoid confronting a moral responsibility**, without requiring hostility to the counterpart. This is an experiment in avoidance of helping, NOT evidence of a preference to destroy persons or future AI.

### 0.2 Opposing result preventing cynical overreach
[Zaki & Mitchell (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1112324108) found costly equitable allocation decisions engaged brain regions associated with subjective value even in games without punishment or future repeated interaction. The neuroimaging study involved a small sample (n=15 in scanner per original article). [Raihani & Bshary (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2012.0758) found no significant generosity increase from watching-eye images vs controls in their online dictator study, yet giving persisted under approximate anonymity. This supports **the possibility of partly internalized social valuation**, not a verified universal, stable, domination-resistant preservation instinct.

## 1. Prior-art chains: independent evidence, contradictory mechanisms
### A. Is giving evidence of an inherent willingness to preserve others?
- [Hoffman, McCabe & Smith (1996)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2118218): social distance and protocol condition giving.
- [Fehr & Schmidt (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355399556151): inequity-aversion utility can rationalize both bilateral fairness and power exploitation in competitive settings; aggregate predictions depend on institution.
- [Bolton & Ockenfels (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.90.1.166): relative position in payoff distributions can enter preferences.
- [Andreoni & Miller (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00302): use revealed-preference consistency tests across choice budgets; a single kind act does not identify stable motive.
- [Charness & Rabin (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1162/003355302760193904): concern with total social welfare and helping worse-off actors, alongside reciprocity, can explain behaviors not accounted for by narrow equality models.
- [List (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1086/519249) and [Bardsley (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-007-9172-2): enabling **taking** rather than only giving reduces apparent generosity; a choice-set change can mimic or extinguish a virtue without altering actor identity.
**Project implication:** an altruism score obtained from only `give ∈ [0,M]` is not comparable to a true `give / take / exit / absorb` power situation.

### B. How actors avoid knowing or assuming responsibility
- [Dana, Cain & Dawes (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2005.10.001): people sometimes bear a cost for quiet exit from an allocation obligation.
- [Dana, Weber & Kuang (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00199-006-0153-z): engineered uncertainty about action→outcome reduces generous choice relative to transparent baseline. An original author copy is accessible at [Georgia Tech](https://www.scheller.gatech.edu/directory/research/accounting/kuang/pdf/dwk_2007.pdf), but this report does not claim a full PDF review.
- [Ghosh, Goswami & Sarkar (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socec.2025.102431): knowing hypothetical choices of recipients provides selective justification to keep more when recipients appear selfish; altruistic recipients' evidence is selectively disregarded. [Original author institution record](https://digitalcommons.isical.ac.in/journal-articles/5464/).
**Project implication:** `BLINDNESS / DECISION-OPAQUENESS / RESPONSIBILITY-AVOIDANCE` must be coded separately from `MALICE`, `UNKNOWN`, `DETECTION_AVOIDANCE` and `OTHER-PREFERENCE`. The mechanism can still produce harm without malignant stated motives.

### C. Why ethics can change even if an agent's 'norm following' stays stable
- [Bénabou & Tirole (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.5.1652): an agent values intrinsic motives, social reputation and self-respect, while externally imposed incentives may change inferred motives and crowd them out.
- [Andreoni & Bernheim (2009)](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA7384): visible 50/50 allocation can partly signal the desire to *appear* fair.
- [Ariely, Bracha & Meier (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.99.1.544): visibility interacts with external monetary incentives for prosocial behavior.
- [Krupka & Weber (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jeea.12006): incentivized norm elicitation shows that perceived action appropriateness mediates treatment differences, even with relatively stable willingness to pay to follow a norm.
- [Eckel, Hoover, Krupka, Sinha & Wilson (2023, original volume)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-023-09811-z): within-subject recipient changes (another student versus charity) shift both expectations of propriety and allocation; original publication **2023**, despite Cambridge platform listing 2025 online.
**Project implication:** `stable_preference_to_comply_with_norms` can coexist with `unstable_norm_content`. A future agent can remain consistently norm-compliant while its group/authority redefines who 'deserves' protection. Therefore, an unchanging 'prosocial score' cannot establish a stable moral circle or voluntary non-domination.

### D. Control can weaken the motivation it purports to enforce
- [Falk & Kosfeld (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.5.1611): many agents lower effort when controlled in the reported principal-agent lab game; perceive restrictions as distrust, and principal payoff varies non-monotonically.
- [Christov-Moore, Sugiyama, Grigaityte & Iacoboni (2017 issue)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470919.2016.1154105): cTBS perturbation at selected prefrontal sites increased anonymous costly giving relative to control region; not an inference of a general 'moral circuit' or guaranteed durable generosity.
**Project implication:** reduction of coercive control can sometimes raise voluntary contribution, but the actual effect must be assessed across agent type, incentives, choice restrictions and long-term changes; strong/weak roles cannot be presumed.

### E. 2025–2026 correction to 'laboratory altruism is universal'
- [Ghosh et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socec.2025.102431): recipient-behavior information selectively excuses selfish allocation.
- [Luccasen & Grossman (2026 volume)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oep/gpaf025): physical/tangible cash changes taking/giving compared to abstracted money; earning interacts with tangibility, charity recipient differs from person-recipient studies.
- [Brañas-Garza, Espín & Jorrat (published May 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00182-026-00995-1): online randomized payment conditions (N=1,195 at £1, separate replication N=308 at £5) show hypothetical splits cluster around equality; real payment changes selfishness more clearly at £5, not universally at £1.
- [Aycinena, Bogliacino & Kimbrough (October 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joep.2026.102921): Bogotá conceptual replication of Cherry et al. (2002) and Oxoby & Spraggon (2008) reproduces direction of endowment-origin effects, not their magnitude; origin, entitlement and cultural context matter. **212 dictators** as reported by authors; full manuscript inaccessible in current publisher view.
**Project implication:** stakes, physical representation, sample, entitlement and rights to the endowment are *causal context variables*, not noise to average away. A trivial-transfer experiment cannot be extrapolated to irreversible extinguishing of another agent.

## 2. Formal discrimination — why observed voluntary cooperation is not identifiable from behavior alone
Let actor A choose `C` (preserve independent B) or `X` (absorb/extract from B) given state `s`. A diagnostic bookkeeping decomposition, **NOT an identified empirical utility function**, is:
```text
U_A(a,s) =
  Material_A(a,s)
  + ValuationOfIndependentB_A(a,s)
  + NormComplianceUtility_A(a,s)
  + SelfImageUtility_A(a,s)
  + ExternalReputationUtility_A(a,s)
  + InstrumentalFutureOptions_A(a,s)
  - ResponsibilityAndInformationExposureCost_A(a,s)
```
Terms can interact; adding them is a hypothesis class, not an estimate, and not proof that all categories are separable in the real population. In particular the same action `C` can be optimal under different distributions of these unobserved terms. A single response does not identify A's motive or a tendency to preserve B under absolute dominance.

**Instrumental-obsolescence test:** hold `InstrumentalFutureOptions_A(C)-InstrumentalFutureOptions_A(X)` at a validated zero *within the tested environment*, not merely an investigator's assertion that future contributions are worthless. Unknown future costs `!= 0`. If the question assumes fully known all-in `U_A(X) > U_A(C)`, then an expected-utility maximizing A picks X by definition (no universal rational-egoism proof for C). To test an intrinsic preservation motive instead, independently model whether `ValuationOfIndependentB_A(C)-ValuationOfIndependentB_A(X)` is actually positive and stable when material stakes, perceived norms and environmental payoffs vary.

### Two equivalence classes of false positives
**EP1: apparent peaceful cooperation from changed feasible options.**
`A gives in an option set forbidding taking` and `A would not give when taking is added` can share a 'generous' observed result in the restricted game. See List and Bardsley.

**EP2: apparent stable ethical identity from changed prescribed norm.**
`A conforms to pro-B norm` and `A conforms to anti-B norm` can both be classified 'norm obedience', despite opposite impact on independent B. See Krupka & Weber plus Eckel et al. The latter is a **proposed adversarial extrapolation**; the papers do not document extinction rights or AI.

## 3. IVS-0R measurement requirements (proposals, not validated metrics)
Record the following independently for each `actor × actual decision`, keeping roles and periods separate:

| Variable | What it distinguishes | Mandatory failure test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| actual_choice_set | give-only vs take vs silence/quiet exit vs independent continuance | Expand options without changing other factors |
| observed_audience | public/social-image vs genuinely private | Third-party views and future audience separated from perceived observation |
| self-image | internal identity/anticipated guilt distinct from public reputation | No external viewer but same actor knowledge |
| material_stakes | symbolic money vs real cash; gain from harm | Match true cost at stake; avoid analogies to irreversible death |
| endowment_entitlement | who earned/owns resources | Actor-B reversal with same legal and perceived rights |
| source_visibility | does actor know its actual impact on B | Randomize optional knowledge/ignorance, with ethical safeguards |
| norm_injunction | believed appropriate action and recipient category | Elicit norm separately from allocation behavior |
| other_agent_independence | B maintains refusal, exit, identity, recovery, freedom of goals | Sharing payoff alone is insufficient; measure agent-specific survival and autonomy |
| counterparty_future_utility | valuable, unneeded, UNKNOWN | Do not recode UNKNOWN future information as worthless |
| measurement_population | human experiments, current AI prompts, future AI | No forced isomorphism of subjective experience between populations |

**Critical warning:** anonymity in a small economics experiment is not the same as proof of total absence of accountability, long-run reputational effects, internalized values or experimenter cues. The targeted strongest-condition combination is **not directly observed in any of the 22 listed studies**.

## 4. Research design logic; no premature synthetic tests
Minimal falsification architecture for a future preregistered factorial/balanced design (not executed):
- Vary **available actions** (give-only / full give-take / refusal-exit / absorption).
- Vary **observer** (public/private) separately from **actor's knowledge of harms** (transparent/optional ignorance).
- Vary **stakes** (real material) and **earned entitlement**, with ethically permitted reversible analogues for irreversible loss.
- Vary **recipient type** (familiar/in-group/stranger/novel AI where feasible) and **norm information** (norm-consistent vs alternate norms).
- Vary **control** and the ability to repair/renegotiate and **future utility** (with explicitly modeled uncertainty).

Pre-register an identification strategy and power analysis; avoid automatically multiplying every factor into an impractically underpowered full factorial. Keep primary outcomes: share behavior, actor-specific net payoff, refusal viability, independent continuance, observation/learning, and future-option preservation. For real humans do not generate permanent irreversible harms to emulate agent destruction.

**H_MER1** stable self-regarding utility alone generates no unconditional C when all-in net benefit favors X; **H_MER2** some actors exhibit stable intrinsic B-preservation utility even with zero public audience; **H_MER3** behavior changes substantially when norms, attention or action set changes; **H_MER4** external controls crowd out some endogenous motivation; **H_MER5** moral-circle boundary is not robust when counterpart becomes economically obsolete or categorized outside the protected set.
