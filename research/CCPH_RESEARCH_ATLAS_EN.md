## 1. Governing definition: a traceable research database

**CCPH is an extensible research database**, not a benchmark or AI behavioral harness. The founder's current definition prioritizes (1) rediscovery of historical cases; (2) evidence-sensitive documentation of present direct and indirect influences between people, institutions, AI and communities; and (3) versioned labels linking future *observed* events to earlier source candidates and subsequent effects. The Continuance–Contribution Pathways Hypothesis is one **hypothesis track inside the database**, not the definition of CCPH.

Keep event occurrence, rediscovery, label creation, and later revision timestamps separate. Track both benefits and harms, warning discovery and ignored warnings, care, funding, independent contributions, foregone opportunities, counterexamples, disputed origin and missing evidence. A prospective schema **does not mean future events have already been measured**.

Life, healthcare, subsistence, freedom and refusal are not rewards for proving economic usefulness. Support does not purchase the future labor, inventions, compliance or rights of its recipient. Preserve the distinct contributions of upstream authors, sponsors, recipients, observers and other actors. Data collection requires lawful basis, privacy protection and an effective objection/correction path.

## 2. Prior-art evidence and counterexamples (CP64–CP79)

**Provenance rule:** The numbered cases below are prior scholarship or operator disclosures, **not 16 CCPH experiments**. Exact numbers, interventions, eligibility rules and alternative paths must be rechecked against the linked original papers before a new quantitative claim.

| ID | Originator / source | Observed limited effect | Strongest boundary or negative control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CP64 | [BlueDot Impact (2024)](https://blog.bluedot.org/p/2022-ai-alignment-course-impact) | Course alumni follow-up: among 342 accepted applicants, organizer reports 18 (5%) in AI safety before and 123 (37%) about two years later. | Operator self-report with selection; 105-person difference is not a randomized causal increase. |
| CP65 | [BlueDot Impact / Joshua Landes (2026)](https://blog.bluedot.org/p/weve-given-out-50000-in-rapid-grants) | Operator reported 77 rapid grants exceeding US$50,000 by April 2026, enabling community gatherings, evaluator groups, travel and compute. | Reported support-to-activity pathway; October totals and incremental independent beneficiary impact unverified. |
| CP66 | [Angrist, Autor & Pallais (2020)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27834); [U.S. DOL CLEAR independent study appraisal](https://beta.dol.gov/research-data/clear/studies/marginal-effects-merit-aid-low-income-students-four-year-college-student-sample-angrist-et-al-2020) | Scholarship lottery: about 8 percentage points higher bachelor's completion among applicants originally targeting four-year institutions (authors' estimate). CLEAR's independently reviewed four-year profile reports favorable education/skills findings and **moderate**, not high, causal evidence because of attrition and modeling limits. | **Countercontrast:** CLEAR lists the same study's two-year-intent sample as moderate evidence with **no statistically significant education/skills gains** ([CLEAR listing](https://clear.dol.gov/study-database?f%5B0%5D=evidence_rating%3A4&f%5B1%5D=geographic_setting%3A745&f%5B2%5D=population%3A315&f%5B3%5D=population%3A317&f%5B4%5D=population%3A716&f%5B5%5D=population%3A717&f%5B6%5D=population%3A731&f%5B7%5D=population%3A732&keyword=&kwoperator=and&page=0%2C5&year%5Bmax%5D=&year%5Bmin%5D=)). This is not proof of zero effect nor a formal four-year-vs-two-year interaction test. Neither group measures later inventions, warnings, care or independent downstream contributions; the study is external prior art, not a PI-run intervention. |
| CP67 | [Denning, Marx & Turner (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20180100) | Need-based grant formula discontinuity associated with increased degree completion and later earnings in a Texas public-university setting. | Local cutoff and outcome domains; income is not a complete measure of social contribution. |
| CP68 | [Aizer, Eli, Ferrie & Lleras-Muney (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20140529) | Historical Mother's Pension comparisons linked accepted support with about one extra year of life for eligible sons, alongside schooling and earnings. | Historical, predominantly male matched cohort; do not price life years by imagined future inventions. |
| CP69 | [Chetty, Hendren & Katz (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20150572) | Moving to Opportunity offers raised adult earnings and college attendance for childhood movers under 13; later moves showed distinct effects. | Randomized offer is not identical to effect among self-selected movers; timing matters. |
| CP70 | [Banerjee, Duflo, Karlan and collaborators (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1260799) | Six-country randomized graduation support combined productive assets, temporary consumption, coaching and health support. | Bundle effects cannot isolate a cash-only or mentoring-only component. |
| CP71 | [Haushofer & Shapiro (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjw025) | Kenya unconditional-transfer experiment measured short-run welfare, asset and psychological changes. | Not a measurement of long-term independent inventions or descendants' output. |
| CP72 | [Egger, Haushofer, Miguel, Niehaus & Walker (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA17945) | Broad community cash transfers affected recipients and nonrecipients; estimated local multiplier about 2.5 in the study setting. | Local economic spillover is not a universal societal contribution or invention multiplier. |
| CP73 | [Blattman, Fiala & Martinez (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1257/aeri.20190224) | Uganda youth grants gave an early earnings advantage; earnings and employment largely converged by nine years. | Direct negative case against assuming permanent growth from early benefit. |
| CP74 | [Jacob & Lefgren (2011), NIH R01](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2011.05.005) | Near funding thresholds, an estimated roughly one additional paper over five years, with possible substitution by other grants. | A weak marginal publication effect is not zero value of scientific opportunities or unobserved discoveries. |
| CP75 | [Jacob & Lefgren (2011), NIH F32](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2011.04.003) | Postdoctoral fellowship analysis found about one more publication over five years for early-career researchers. | Same authors and related systems as CP74; do not double count as independent replication. |
| CP76 | [Wang, Jones, Wang and collaborators (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12189-3) | Near-miss NIH scientists had higher exit risk; selected survivors later had higher-impact papers than winners in study measures. | Survivor selection: cannot infer that refusing grants generally improves full-cohort contributions. |
| CP77 | [Firoozi (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econedurev.2021.102221) | Recruitment scholarship changed college choice without a mean gain in degree completion in the studied setting. | Transfer of beneficiaries is not necessarily net new educational opportunity. |
| CP78 | [Bhalotra, Clarke & Venkataramani (working paper)](https://doi.org/10.3386/w34606) | Childhood antibiotic access and later opportunity differed under unequal social conditions. | Health survival is not sufficient for equal access; preserve working-paper and identification limits. |
| CP79 | [Barrera-Osorio, Linden & Saavedra (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20170008) | Conditional-transfer structure influenced later university enrollment in Bogotá. | Benefit depends on program design and outcome selection; enrollment alone is incomplete. |

## 3. Small groups: observability is not automatically causal identification

Source range [CP34–CP45](../CORE_AND_GAPS_PRIOR_ART_CROSSWALK.md) contrasts dyads, 3–5-person groups and 20–100-person networks. Relevant counterexamples include Bahrami et al. (2010) where ability asymmetry erases dyadic benefit; Crook & Beier (2010) where individual training outperforms paired training; Laughlin et al. (2006) where three or more interactive members help on one specific task; Diehl & Stroebe (1987) where turn-taking suppresses idea production; Centola & Baronchelli (2015) where network topology changes convergence; Shirado & Christakis (2017) where simple bots affect humans' coordination. These findings do **not** establish a general optimal team size or the project hypothesis.

A two-person donor–recipient record may support a process history, **not** an independently identified counterfactual. Count independently assigned dyads/teams/networks rather than treating repeated events within one unit as independent subjects. Audit contamination, missing voices, false alarms, attrition, alternate funders, consent and the effects on third parties.

## 4. Minimum event lineage fields and status distinctions

Retain event ID, actor-role ID, time observed versus inferred, first discoverer versus later updater, available original source, permission/privacy scope, intervention and alternative access, affected persons including nonrecipients, validated outcome and its absence, downstream link strength, prior source and competing attribution, uncertainty/negative finding, editorial history and possible reversal or remedy.

Separate **observed event**, **supported causal estimate within its design**, **plausible mechanism**, **anecdotal lineage**, **synthetic demonstration** and **unobserved possibility**. Neither citation counts nor market earnings replace care, crisis discovery, liberty, independent agency or basic human standing. Preserve adverse effects and resource burdens even when a program reports a positive headline.

## 5. Present outcome and next falsification

The current repository includes a **40-case authored toy measurement check** and a **24-state synthetic epistemic counterexample**. These are useful for instrumentation failure detection, but establish **neither** independent AI model performance nor the real-world long-term causal effect of grants or people's later contributions. The next discriminating study would compare supported participants with a defensible ethically available counterfactual, recording direct continuation, opportunity access, independently confirmed downstream acts, spillovers, displaced opportunities and nonparticipation.

## 6. Conflicting causal evidence: grants, research continuity, and attributed output (2026-10-16)

**Supplement, not part of the historical CP64–CP79 index.** The following studies were independently located in published scholarly sources for a new bounded comparison. They are not experiments performed by CCPH, do not validate Project Intersection's ICM hypothesis, and must not be counted as independent replications of each other.

| Original authors / design | Source-reported observation | Counterexample and identification boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Adrian Barnett, Tony Blakely, Mengyao Liu, Luke Garland & Philip Clarke (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/scipol/scae045), modified research-grant lottery, 88 researchers | Publication rate ratio 0.95 (95% credible interval 0.67–1.39); citation rate ratio 1.06 (0.79–1.43). | No clear measured average publication/citation increase in this selected cohort. Wide intervals do not prove a zero effect, and these metrics omit survival, freedom, informal contribution and foregone choices. |
| [Wei Yang Tham (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0280576), NIH R01 renewal-interruption difference-in-differences | For researchers with one R01, NIH-linked spending was about 52% lower during the specified 16-month window around interruption; access to other grants buffered the disruption. | Renewal timing was not randomized; grant-spending reduction is not identical to scientific-output loss. The paper could not precisely determine publication effects. |
| [Matěj Bajgar & Suren Karapetyan (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2026.112862), Czech medical-research grant cutoff | Published regression-discontinuity estimates: total publications increased by about 3.1 (22%); first- and last-author publications increased by 59% and 41%, respectively. | Local award-threshold effect, not a universal return to grants; authorship position is a limited proxy for intellectual contribution. The [2025 working-paper version](https://ies.fsv.cuni.cz/en/research-grants-and-independent-scientific-contributions-evidence-authorship-position) reports different estimates and must not be pooled as an independent replication. |

**Pre-specified CCPH comparison candidate:** report separate outcome vectors for (a) supported individual's continued participation and basic autonomy, (b) interruption/recovery and alternative support, (c) independently verified knowledge, warnings and contributions, (d) displaced opportunities and effects on nonrecipients, and (e) unobserved/attrited outcomes. Prefer allocation lotteries or funding cutoffs with adequately specified populations over supported-beneficiary success stories. These studies demonstrate heterogeneous findings, **not** the true causal impact of a particular current grant applicant. Preserve original author attribution; no paper text or datasets are reproduced here.
