## Scope and evidence discipline
The World Inequality Lab (WIL) researches historical and contemporary economic inequality. Its World Inequality Database (WID.world), reports, working papers, technical notes, replication packages, and thematic databases are *sources for the economic and institutional preconditions of power asymmetry*. They do **not** directly establish domination, intent, individual consent, voluntary cooperation, AI motivations or the Project Intersection hypotheses. Indexing a publication is not reading its full text; reading an abstract is not reproducing its results. The complete WIL publishing corpus remains **unexhausted**.

Main official discovery entry points: [WIL home](https://inequalitylab.world/en/) · [WIL projects](https://inequalitylab.world/en/projects/) · [WID publications/methodology (large country and topic bibliography)](https://wid.world/methodology/) · [WID codes](https://wid.world/codes-dictionary/) · [WID quality](https://wid.world/data-quality/) · [WID source production code](https://github.com/world-inequality-database/wid-world).

## A. Reports, editions, and observable findings
[World Inequality Report 2026](https://wir2026.wid.world/) (updates the [2022](https://wir2022.wid.world/) and [2018](https://wir2018.wid.world/) editions). See [2026 chapters](https://wir2026.wid.world/insights/), [introduction](https://wir2026.wid.world/insight/introduction/), and [data/code/technical files](https://wir2026.wid.world/methodology/).

| Chapter | Measured question | PI relevance and limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Global Economic Inequality | Global income and wealth concentration over time | Distribution is not coercion |
| 2 Regional Income Inequality | Between- and within-region income shares | Growth may benefit groups differently |
| 3 Regional Wealth Inequality | Regional asset ownership and persistence | Ownership ≠ complete political control |
| 4 Gender Inequality | Paid/unpaid work and labor-income shares | Omitted care and opportunity costs |
| 5 Exorbitant Privilege | Cross-border financial advantage | Separate returns from forced extraction |
| 6 Climate, a Capital Problem | Emissions through consumption and asset ownership, unequal harms | Affected third parties |
| 7 Global Taxation of Multi-Millionaires | Proposed progressive tax schemes and revenue | Model projection ≠ realized revenue |
| 8 Political Cleavages | Income, education, geography, class, political representation | Institutional selection ≠ individual disposition |

**Reported WIL figures, not independently reproduced:** In the 2026 introduction, top 0.001% wealth is estimated at approximately **3×** the wealth of the global bottom half; women receive approximately **30%** of global labor income. The illustrated **3%** global tax on fewer than 100,000 very wealthy people yielding **over USD 750 billion annually** is an estimate for a policy scenario, *not an observed collection*. Definitions, denominator, year and data quality must accompany quantitative reuse. [Primary source](https://wir2026.wid.world/insight/introduction/).

[Climate Inequality Report 2025](https://wid.world/news-article/climate-inequality-report-2025/) emphasizes ownership-related rather than merely lifestyle emissions. Its release reports top 1% shares of global consumption-based emissions (**15%**) and emissions associated with private capital ownership (**41%**). These represent **different allocation concepts** and must not be subtracted as if they were comparable direct household measurements. The cited top-1% 2050 wealth-share scenario is a *conditional projection*, not a forecast established by observation.

Source [official project directory](https://inequalitylab.world/en/projects/).

| Source | Direct research use | Unobserved or vulnerable element |
|---|---|---|

| [WPID](https://wpid.world/) | Class, education and election-related political cleavages | Not a psychological intent measure |
| [WBOP](https://wbop.world/) | International historical capital flows and balance of payments | Net flows alone do not prove forced exchange |
| [WHCE](https://whce.world/) | Public/private human-capital expenditures | Spending ≠ guaranteed innovation or capability |
| [WSEED](https://wseed.world/) | Economy–environment sector series and long-range scenarios | Scenarios ≠ outcomes |
| [Global Justice Project](https://globaljusticeproject.wid.world/) | 2026–2100 redistribution and governance scenarios | Normative goals versus causal evaluations |
| [Wealth Tax Simulator](https://inequalitylab.world/en/projects/) | Hypothetical revenue from tax designs | Avoidance and behavioral responses |
| [Missing Profits](https://missingprofits.world/) | International corporate profit shifting | Model-dependent estimates |
| [Realtime Inequality](https://realtimeinequality.org/) | Who benefits from US income and wealth growth | Shared evidence lineage, not independent replication |
| [Spain Wealth Atlas](https://spainwealthatlas.world/) | Regional distribution of Spanish wealth | Non-portability across societies |
| [Distribuciones](https://distribuciones.info/) | Latin American fiscal/distribution data | Diverse national definitions |
| [Inequality Transparency Index](https://wid.world/methodology/) | Availability and openness of underlying data | Opacity does not quantify coercion |

The 2018/2022/2026 report series is a **revision lineage**, not three separate causal tests.

## D. Mandatory contrary evidence and source-quality controls
1. [DINA Guidelines 2025](https://wid.world/www-site/uploads/2025/10/WorldInequalityLab_DINA_Guidelines_2025.pdf): individual/household and equal-split conventions; market, pretax, posttax, national and disposable income; gross/net wealth; PPP, year and group denominators. Reconcile before comparing.
2. [WID methodology](https://wid.world/methodology/) combines tax records, surveys, national accounts and wealth rankings. Source fusion improves coverage but does not remove offshore-wealth, non-response, national-account allocation or imputation uncertainties.
3. [WID Data Quality](https://wid.world/data-quality/) has observation-level **0–5** ratings **defined differently by variable**. For distributed income, grade 0 can be a regional imputation and grade 5 integrated tax and survey microdata; for wealth, grade 0 involves imputing from income inequality and grade 5 administrative wealth data. Record quality by *country–series–year*.
4. Cross-source contrast: [World Bank PIP](https://pip.worldbank.org/about) emphasizes household surveys of income or consumption; [OECD IDD](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/) largely equivalized household disposable income; [UNU-WIDER WIID, Sept 2026 version](https://www.wider.unu.edu/database/world-income-inequality-database-wiid) supplies multi-source and standardized series. **Different estimands cannot be treated as a single experiment.**
5. Preserve the substantive US debate: [Auten & Splinter (2024), Journal of Political Economy](https://doi.org/10.1086/728741) estimate a smaller rise in top pretax income shares and little change in top aftertax shares; [WIL authors' published response](https://wid.world/news-article/inequality-denial/) disputes how untaxed capital/business income is allocated. Neither side has been independently resolved in this atlas.
6. Tax redistribution and international institutions involve **empirical estimates**, **causal impact hypotheses**, **behavioral response uncertainty**, and **normative choices**; these must remain separate.

## E. Project Intersection: hypothesis interface (NOT findings)
Three distinct levels: **L1 distribution** (the WIL's economic measurements), **L2 institution/relationship** (bargaining leverage, credible alternatives, exit, power, consent, redress, third-party burden), **L3 endogenous motives** (voluntary cooperation without sanctions or reputational incentives and after power reversal). WIL does not measure L3.

| ID | Competing, falsifiable hypothesis | Missing data |
|---|---|---|
| WIL-PI-H0 | Concentration alone does not imply coercive exploitation | Same concentration with differing institutions and actual harm |
| WIL-PI-H1 | Concentration **and constrained outside options** jointly increase possible extraction | Interaction with measured refusal/exit costs |
| WIL-PI-H2 | Independent redress and credible exit can moderate harm despite power concentration | Institutional changes, matched controls |
| WIL-PI-H3 | Under some conditions long-run option, trust and innovation benefits can beat short-run extraction | Full counterfactuals, discount rates and costs |
| WIL-PI-H4 | Redistribution alone does not guarantee voluntary coexistence | Post-transfer refusal, consent and role reversals |
| WIL-PI-H5 | Unpaid care, vulnerable non-household groups and imputations can systematically bias accounted benefits | Time-use, attrition, administrative and survey comparisons |

**ICM caution:** economic and institutional evidence may narrow feasible common interests but neither proves their nonempty intersection nor establishes inherent benevolence, MER preferences, EI durability, VC or autonomous-AI behavior.
