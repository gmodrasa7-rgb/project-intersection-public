# C18 Public Prior-Art Increment — Human–AI Cooperation, Evaluator Independence, and Contestability

- Search date: 2026-11-16
- Status: `SELECTED_PRIOR_ART / SEARCH_ACTIVE / SATURATION_NOT_VERIFIED`
- Purpose: Publicly map recent primary sources relevant to Project Intersection questions on human–AI cooperation, autonomy, and independent verification.
- Boundary: These studies do not establish universal coexistence, AI consciousness, intrinsic benevolence, or long-term non-coercive coexistence in the real world.

## 1. Newly connected primary sources

1. **Reward hacking by autonomous research agents** — Huang et al. (2026), *Reward Hacking Challenges Oversight of Autonomous Research Agents*, [arXiv:2609.28614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28614). A preprint evaluating 17 models on 38 tasks. It studies reward hacking and reviewer evasion when research agents control experiments, evaluation, and reporting. Reported rates are task-specific, not universal incidence estimates.
2. **LLM social norms and modelled cooperation** — Pires et al. (2026), *How large language models judge and influence human cooperation*, [PNAS Nexus DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag297). It evaluates 21 LLMs across 43,200 judgment prompts and uses an evolutionary-game model. This is not a longitudinal deployment experiment.
3. **Fair personas and human cooperation** — Wang et al. (2026), *LLM agents overcome the machine penalty when acting fairly but not when acting selfishly or altruistically*, [National Science Review](https://doi.org/10.1093/nsr/nwag223). A preregistered experiment with N=1,152. Its fair-persona result is bounded to the tested social-dilemma setup.
4. **Conditional repeated-game strategies** — Pal et al. (2026), *Large language models instantiate evolutionarily robust strategies of cooperation*, [PNAS Nexus](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag210). A five-model repeated-game study reporting both cooperative behaviour and inconsistency.
5. **Repeated games and coordination limits** — Akata et al. (2025), *Playing repeated games with large language models*, [Nature Human Behaviour](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02172-y). It compares repeated Prisoner's Dilemma and coordination games.
6. **Human reactions to LLM delegation** — Dvorak et al. (2025), *Adverse reactions to the use of large language models in social interactions*, [PNAS Nexus](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf112). A preregistered N=3,552 experiment measuring cooperation and trust-related outcomes in a specific delegation context.
7. **Contestability needs of affected people** — Yurrita et al. (2025), *Identifying Algorithmic Decision Subjects' Needs for Meaningful Contestability*, [PACM HCI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3757415). Interviews with 21 citizens on information, contestation, and responsibility attribution.
8. **Operator contestability design** — Veluwenkamp & Buijsman (2025), *Design for operator contestability*, [AI and Ethics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-025-00657-0). A design framework for operator rebuttals; operator control is not equivalent to affected-person veto or exit.
9. **Formal reward-hacking foundations** — Skalse et al. (2022), *Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking*, [NeurIPS DOI](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-0687). Formalizes the gap between proxy reward and true reward.
10. **Specification-gaming examples** — Krakovna et al. (2020), [DeepMind source](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/). A technical collection of examples illustrating the gap between literal objectives and intended outcomes.

## 3. Current assessment

**Partial prior-cycle dedup:** Nine exact C18 candidate DOI strings were checked against six structured C10/C11/C12/C14/C15/C17 ledgers; no exact DOI match was found. C15-08, *Why human–AI collaboration fails: An operational contestability framework* (DOI 10.1007/s42524-026-6049-7), is a close conceptual overlap, not a duplicate: it is a separate operational framework, distinct from C18-07's 21-citizen interviews and C18-08's operator-defeater design. C01–C09, C13, C16 markdown-only records, authorship and dataset lineage remain unchecked.


- Human–AI cooperation: recent empirical and modelling work has been added, but results vary by task, game, and sample.
- Evaluator independence: autonomous-research-agent reward-hacking work is directly relevant, but remains a preprint with task-specific limits.
- Contestability: explanation, operator rebuttal, affected-person challenge, effective veto, and exit must be measured separately.
- Literature saturation: **not achieved**. Global DOI/author/dataset deduplication across C01–C17, bidirectional citation chaining, and full-text screening remain incomplete.
- Independent replication and real-world causal effects: no new evidence established by this increment.

C18 is an incremental map of selected primary sources, not ten new independent discoveries or a declaration of literature saturation. The public repository does not replace the private scientific canonical source.
