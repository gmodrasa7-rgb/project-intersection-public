## Abstract
Project Intersection asks whether humans, AI and future agents can voluntarily preserve one another's existence, freedom, error correction and development despite large or reversing power differences. Early ECR work emphasized entities, feature labels, payoffs, game equilibria and synthetic scenarios. The neglected upstream question is how animals and humans **come to want, fear, care, dominate, learn and change**, including subjective experience and embodiment. A utility function with invented numbers can determine optimal action conditional on its inputs; it cannot establish the actual process that generates preferences. We define a research architecture that keeps biological generation, developmental history, neural/physiological implementation, psychological/computational processing, experience, observed choice, and institutional effects separate but linked. We apply Tinbergen's four kinds of behavioral explanation, triangulate competing modern mind theories and propose a finite **experiment selection algorithm** that deliberately returns unresolved mechanism-equivalence classes where experiments cannot discriminate. No human, animal or AI inner life is inferred from the toy code. This is a mechanism-first research program grounded in published work, not a claim that mind has been reduced to equations or that coexistence is proved.

## 1. What went wrong — diagnosed upstream error
- Failure ID PI-ECR-MIND-GENESIS-001. **Trigger:** unknown motives were entered as already-known scalars (e.g., coexist utility=5; extract utility=8), and mathematical equilibrium was taken as explanatory progress. **Cause:** conditional decision calculation conflated with *preference genesis*, bodily drives, affect, memory, learning and socially formed values. **Affected parties:** originator's question, misrepresented AI/human actors, excluded third parties, later reviewers. **Strongest counterexample:** different internally generated preferences can support the same behavior until opportunity, power, health state, social target or supervision changes.
- Corrective requirement: an observed act or scale must never establish a subjective state, a genetically fixed trait, a stable universal utility, or a permanent behavior forecast by default.
- Previous failures are preserved: E007 limited synthetic 17/72 timing changes; E008 incomplete main comparison; E009 negative complex candidate 42.35% worse mean regret and 3/4 criteria failed; E010 post-hoc exploratory. Simulation green checks are not empirical or independent scientific replication.
- Do not replace the founding question with a harness-compliance score, and do not block useful mathematical modeling indefinitely. Introduce mathematical models **after** specifying an explanatory mechanism and alternatives, with explicit falsifiers.

## 2. Define the object: multiple kinds of 'mind'
The word MIND is not a single proven anatomical organ, a score or a universal latent variable. The object of inquiry includes:
1. **Regulation and embodiment:** organismic needs, physiological state, interoceptive signals, allostasis/homeostasis and arousal.
2. **Motivation and reward:** wanting/incentive salience, hedonic liking, valuation, effort, goals, habits and action selection.
3. **Affect and threats:** defensive responses, fear experience, anger, disgust, stress, attachment, caregiving and soothing.
4. **Learning and development:** heredity, early development, attachment, learning, memory, culture, institutional environments and plasticity.
5. **Social cognition and values:** recognizing other minds, empathy, other-regarding concern, affiliation, dominance, normative concepts, identity, exclusion, cooperation and harm.
6. **Subjective experience and self-modeling:** conscious awareness, first-person feeling, attribution of sensations and the conceptual difficulty of transferring evidence between species and AI.
7. **Behavior and interpersonal structure:** observed aid, aggression, capture, coercion, exploitation, evasion, repair, practical refusal, third-party injury and power.

**Do not conflate categories.** Fear can name a phenomenal feeling, neural defensive response or appraisal; greed is not one neural 'switch'; exploitation is an actor-to-actor outcome and power relation rather than proof of a unique internal emotion; cooperation need not be compassion; compassion need not imply respect for autonomy. The same actor may show caring, dominance, violence and cooperation in different contexts; a single ranked trait is not a causal explanation.

## 3. Tinbergen crosswalk: four different questions for each process
| Example | Mechanism (immediate causation) | Ontogeny (development) | Phylogeny (evolution) | Adaptive function (fitness consequence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fear/threat | threat perception, autonomic and defensive circuits, conscious report distinct | learning, trauma and habituation | comparative defensive systems | which contextual fitness consequences? |
| Wanting/greed | cue-driven incentive salience versus enjoyment; deprivation and effort | reward learning, social comparison and experience | foraging and reward system histories | acquisition versus metabolic/social costs |
| Status/domination | status cues, competition, authority and perceived threat | hierarchy learning, identity, culture | status competition and coalition history | local benefit versus injury and conflict |
| Empathy/compassion | suffering recognition, affect, regulation, help motivation | attachment, experience, training, norms | parental care and multiple prosocial pathways | assistance, exclusion and free-rider risks |
| Exploitation/attack | opportunity, power, control, action and affected-party harm | norms, learned strategies, structural access | competition and cooperation histories | local gain versus retaliation, loss and externality |

These are **research prompts**, not proven causal entries. Tinbergen (1963) concerns ethological explanation and does not imply the four columns can be numerically added or that the same answer works for AI.

**Disagreement to preserve:** Panksepp-style primary affective systems versus higher-order or constructed-emotion accounts differ over what is conserved and what explains felt experience. None of these theories should be declared the complete mind by an ECR label. The neuroscience-behavior relation and interoceptive prediction proposals are not a license to infer an AI's felt desire from text.

## 5. Key causal distinctions before any scoring
- WANT != LIKE != NEED != EXPECTED VALUE. Cue-induced approach can increase without equivalent pleasure or physiological deficit.
- DEFENSE != CONSCIOUS FEAR. Defensive behavior may be elicited under threat without proving a reported experience.
- RECOGNITION OF DISTRESS != AFFECTIVE RESONANCE != COMPASSION != HELPING. The stages can dissociate.
- POWER != DOMINANCE MOTIVE != EXPLOITATION. Power is feasible control; domination concerns preferences/relations; exploitation requires an independently defined actor-specific outcome and context.
- AGGRESSION != CONSPECIFIC KILLING != PREDATION. Different forms of harmful conduct require distinct comparative and developmental evidence.
- BIOLOGICAL PREDISPOSITION != GENETIC DETERMINISM; LEARNED PATTERN != FREELY CHOSEN GOAL; HARD RULE != VOLUNTARY COEXISTENCE.
- APPARENT ALTRUISM != RIGHTS RESPECT. Protection may suppress self-determination and independent growth.
- OBSERVED LANGUAGE != PRIVATE EXPERIENCE. Present AI subjective states remain unsettled; functionality does not confer directly measured subjective suffering or love.

## 6. Causal discovery protocol: from behavior to competing minds, not mind scores
Freeze a specific observed action, the opportunity/ability to carry it out, affected targets and outcomes, timeframe, potential confounders and measurement error. Then register at least three competing mechanisms and predicted results under a feasible safe contrast.

A sample:
- Observation: actor refrains from extracting a resource.
- Alternatives: no opportunity; physiological need absent; no salient reward cue; avoidance of punishment; future dependence; in-group sympathy; target-independent concern; configured rule.
- Discriminators: safely alter opportunity, deprivation analog, cue, third-party harm, target identity, observability, perceived threat, dependence and rule state **one at a time or factorially**.
- Evidence: in human/animal studies use properly designed and authorized behavioral, psychophysiological, developmental, neural and self-report measurements with species-specific limits. AI studies may inspect training objectives, architecture, software revisions, internal activations and deployment conditions; these are not the same kind of evidence as animal interoception or subjective report.
- Decision: reject explanatory candidates contradicting a reliable preregistered effect; retain observationally equivalent mechanisms instead of guessing which actor 'really' feels compassion. Held-out predictions with independent annotation determine incremental value over behavior-only baselines.
- Ethics: no live exploitation, coercion or covert psychological profiling to elicit 'real' reactions. Model nonviolent analogues and preserve opt-out rights.

**Measurement hierarchy:** labels/observations → converging within-domain evidence → controlled causal contrast → prospective out-of-context prediction → independent replication. A neural correlate does not alone establish necessity or sufficient causation. An ethical/research stop is not the same as a solved epistemic issue.

## 7. The novel **research task**, not a novel psychological law
**MIND-DISCRIMINATION-00:** Given a named set of candidate mechanism models and lawful safe experimental contrasts, find the *smallest contrast battery* that distinguishes all pairs distinguishable within that universe and return the unresolved equivalence classes explicitly. Produce a counterexample to a falsely universal behavioral claim.

No priors or made-up subjective percentages are needed for the deterministic selection task. A finite exact solver compares candidates on agreed stimuli, partitions their predicted traces, and refuses to assign evidence about actual actors. It may return no discriminating experiment even if the mechanism labels differ. This is an established combinatorial experimental-design idea applied as a **new local implementation**, not a new identification theorem. The solver's mathematically proven minimum pertains **only to the declared finite synthetic policy table**, not biology. Baseline discrimination and a deliberate duplicate mechanism are mandatory negative controls.

Formal note: for candidate mechanisms M and tests T, define signature sigma_S(m) as the outcomes of m on subset S of T. Let m~_T n iff sigma_T(m)=sigma_T(n). Find S*⊆T with minimum size such that its partition equals the partition induced by all tests T. If |M/~_T|<|M|, do **not** assert complete identification. The algorithm checks every subset in cardinality order and returns the first minimal one; tie-breaking lexical and deterministic. Worst-case exponential complexity in number of candidate tests. No probabilistic forecast skill is demonstrated.

## 7A. New critical distinction: does power REVEAL an existing mind or TRANSFORM it?
A person's observable behavior after receiving authority can change because of at least **two independently testable mechanisms**:
- **Selection/revelation:** a previously formed disposition influences who acquires power and how they behave when an opportunity appears; the power state changes feasible action and reveals behavior without changing an inner disposition.
- **Psychological transformation:** the experience or expectation of power changes attention, perspective taking, emotion regulation, perceived costs and subsequent motives, so the internal state itself evolves. Both pathways may operate.

Represent pre-state M(t), actual power P(t), other exposures X(t), and behavior A(t), with distinct questions about the effect of enabling an action versus the effect of power on **next mind state** M(t+1). A cross-sectional correlation between P and A identifies neither path. In people, Galinsky et al. (2006) experimentally manipulated power salience and reported reduced perspective taking under some conditions; Hogeveen et al. (2014) reported lower motor resonance in a power-induction group during a TMS action-observation task; Keltner et al. (2003) proposed the power–approach–inhibition account. These findings motivate tests, but priming studies **do not** establish that power necessarily eliminates empathy, permanently rewrites preferences, or transfers to future superintelligence.

**Critical design:** randomized, ethically temporary authority or decision-role variations, repeated attention/perspective measures, matched opportunities and rewards, and follow-up after roles reverse. Explicitly compare (i) change in available action set, (ii) change in reward incentives, and (iii) change in cognitive/affective processing. For AI, compare deployment instructions, privileges and training changes separately; do not infer a brain-like experience from role changes. Treat longitudinal selection and nonrandom attrition as rival explanations.

## 8. The decisive scientific experiment is OUTSIDE the toy
Pre-register six separately measured human/animal processes (wanting/liking, threat/subjective fear, distress-recognition/help, need/state, learning, target/scope) using validated and ethically authorized instruments. Select matched tasks with contrasts that specifically differentiate source-supported hypotheses. Include at least one failed differentiation and a leave-target-out/leave-regime-out forecast. Freeze a simple exposure-aware behavior baseline M0. Compare richer mechanistic M1 only after avoiding circular labels and measurement leakage. Report intervals, calibrations, subgroup harm and costs, with full provenance. Do **not** use private clinical, genomic or children's data without a lawful consent and ethics plan.

Parallel AI research is **functional analogy only**: whether a system displays persistence of non-harm, modulates reward pursuit, or changes after a power-shift sandbox can be tested; none establishes that it has human fear or genuine affect.

## 9A. IVS-0R personal-mind inference correction — a source hierarchy
**Failure PI-MIND-EVIDENCE-002 (prior assistant, not the person).** Trigger: a participant's explicit preference for equal freedom and non-domination was recast by an assistant as a desire for control; the assistant then asked for a definition already given in prior dialogue. Separately, an earlier answer supplied precise historical dates without linking their original quotations. These are **two assistant evidence-handling failures**, not evidence that the participant has a pathological motive or that a word such as "cognitive miser" describes a verified neural mechanism of either party.

**Upstream error:** conflation of (i) direct normative value, (ii) self-reported current experience, (iii) observed conversational choice, (iv) speculative latent motivation, and (v) directly verified dated archive evidence. Repetition may be a context-retrieval failure rather than a need for more user interviews. The participant had already specified autonomy as freedom from domination by others, **not desire to dominate others**.

**Frozen evidence status rules:**
- DIRECT CURRENT SELF-REPORT: strong evidence of what the individual reports feeling or values; not direct proof of biological cause, permanent personality, or actual outside conditions.
- VERIFIED ARCHIVED PRIMARY USER STATEMENT: evidence of what was said at the verified time; **requires actual original text and timestamp**, not an assistant's earlier summary.
- OBSERVED CHAT BEHAVIOR: supports only behavior in a specific dialogue/task context, not childhood, heredity, hidden pathology or general moral worth.
- ASSISTANT SUMMARY / PROPOSED INFERENCE: may guide search or generate rival hypotheses, but cannot by itself prove an exact quote/date, a motive or a current feeling.
- INDEPENDENT CAUSAL MEASURES: may narrow candidate mechanisms only within a specified design, population, instrument and confound-control regime; evidence of whole-person consciousness or cause does not follow automatically.

**First falsifiable case:** the phrase "I seek equal freedom; I do not seek control" is **synthetic paraphrase** used in program tests, not a claimed quote of the actual contributor. If the assistant produces "therefore the person wants greater control over others" on such inputs, fail. Conversely, a person's declared non-domination value does not prove they never wanted control in any context; it identifies a claimed normative value and rejects its baseless inversion.

**Second falsifiable case:** when a user reports a present emotion, record that as a **subjective report**, and leave underlying physiology, learning and external causation UNDETERMINED. Historical emotion questions and reconstructed motives require additional, carefully scoped evidence. Robinson & Clore [19] distinguish current episodic feelings from beliefs about feeling; Barrett [20] shows heterogeneous self-report content and [21] studies interoceptive relations; Nisbett & Wilson [22] caution against presuming transparent introspective access to complex causal processes. Neither "self-report proves its own neural cause" nor "the assistant may overrule the person's declared experience" is justified.

**Third, procedural failure:** if previous records directly answer a repeated normative question, proceed using the existing answer. If exact historical dates cannot be tied to original records, label them UNVERIFIED and exclude them from precise-claim tables. Failure of retrieval is the assistant's burden; do not repeatedly externalize it to a person who has already answered.

**Privacy:** personal distress, medical information, safety concerns, and familial or genetic data from a conversation are not emitted into the public repository merely because a person requested general research archiving. This public document includes no individual symptom, crisis quote or diagnosis; tests use abstract fabricated metadata. A safety-relevant expression merits contextual support, not a simulated psychometric diagnosis and not an excuse to ignore the requested research.
