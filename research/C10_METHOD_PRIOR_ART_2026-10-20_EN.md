## 1. Exit × sanction interactions already have published priority
**Bravo & Squazzoni (2013), _Exit, Punishment and Rewards in Commons Dilemmas: An Experimental Study_.** [Author-published full text and six-arm table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3731340/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0069871) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23936356/).
- **Original experiment:** 144 human participants; public-goods task with `Exit(yes/no) × Incentive(null/reward/fine)`, **six treatment groups**, 10 introductory rounds then 10 treatment rounds. Original abstract: fines were stronger than rewards alone, while rewards benefited more from voluntary participation.
- **Confounding interpretations:** Exit changes group composition and distribution of payoffs; frames and expectations may change. **Opting out of a commons ≠ B's independently executable right to refuse A's encroachment or preserve separate existence.**
- **PI boundary:** Prior credit for factorial exit/sanction designs; unanswered PI estimands are A's **actual attempted harmful action**, B's **effective blocking step**, and separate continuance after **actual** power reversal. No extrapolation to intrinsically protective superintelligence.
- **Source scope:** full original HTML/table and independently checked PubMed metadata; raw data not reanalysed.

## 2. Negative replication lineage: the net effect of control is conditional
**Falk & Kosfeld (2006), _The Hidden Costs of Control_** [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.5.1611) · [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v96y2006i5p1611-1630.html): Experimental principal–agent minimum-performance control often reduced agent motivation; net effect was non-monotonic. It does **not** establish that all control is inefficient.

**Ziegelmeyer, Schmelz & Ploner (2012), _Hidden costs of control: four repetitions and an extension_** [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-011-9302-8) · [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/expeco/v15y2012i2p323-340.html): Four repetitions with **476 subjects** mostly found hidden control costs, but, unlike the original, these costs were often **too small to overturn positive economic incentives**. An extension with 228 subjects under hypothetical incentive conditions differed. Preserve the conflicting magnitude claim: motivational cost ≠ guaranteed reversal of A's net utility. Journal **2012**, even if a later hosting site displays a 2025 online migration date.

## 3. Strategic ignorance, demand effects, partial identification
**Dana, Weber & Kuang (2007)** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00199-006-0153-z) · [author manuscript](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Exploiting_Moral_Wiggle_Room_Experiments_Demonstrating_an_Illusory_Preference_for_Fairness/6571121): Fair experimental choices decreased when outcomes for others were obscured. **Already C02**.

**Larson & Capra (2009)** [original full text](https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/journal/9814/jdm9814.html) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1930297500004009): Reproduced the direction of Dana et al. in double-blind conditions addressing competing button-effort explanations. Does **not** uniquely identify all latent motives. Publication 2009; a 2023 hosting timestamp is **not** the study year.

**de Quidt, Haushofer & Roth (2018)** [AEA](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20171330) · [Princeton](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/measuring-and-bounding-experimenter-demand/) · [2017 NBER version](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23470): Deliberately manipulate perceived researcher wishes and derive demand-robust **bounds**; the bounds are **task-specific**, not proof demand effects vanish.

**Manski (1990), _Nonparametric Bounds on Treatment Effects_** [AER/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/aecrev/v80y1990i2p319-23.html) · [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2006592): Prior original work on treatment-effect bounds for incomplete counterfactual data. Bibliographic/abstract metadata checked; no claim to have audited all formulas or estimated numeric PI bounds.

**Nikiforakis, Oechssler & Shah (2014)** [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2013.06.008) · [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v97y2014icp155-168.html): In a bounded human hierarchy, exploitation was attempted in both information treatments, but **successful** only under incomplete junior information. **Already C10-05**. Effective B resistance is not evidence of A's intrinsic restraint.
