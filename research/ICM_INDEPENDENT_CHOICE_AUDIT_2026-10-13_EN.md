## 1. Reconstructed question, not a predetermined answer
Project Intersection investigates when different actors pursuing their own interests can preserve one another's survival, meaningful choices, independent contributions, fair correction processes and future development, even when relative capabilities and authority change. **Normative aim:** protect each party's minimum viability and proportional safeguards against actual harm. **Empirical question:** under which incentives and conditions does voluntary cooperation outperform locally beneficial extraction? Neither universal cooperation nor inevitable conflict is asserted.

The human founder raised the importance of practical freedom, the difference between spontaneous independent decisions and constrained replay, and a possible accessible, verifiable infrastructure for work, research and credit. Infrastructure is **one potentially useful application**, not the ICM definition or the research's final objective. Assistance with conceptual formalization and presentation by LLMs must not erase human-originated questions or earlier scholars' work.

## 2. The centralization paradox: a logical counterexample
Let an actor's feasible, meaningful activities under institution M be A_i(M). Assume every activity requires authorization by a single operator C, C can deny all requests, and no independent execution, appeal with timely effect, or recovery is available. Then one decision by C can reduce A_i(M) to the empty set. This is **a conditional structural implication**, not evidence that any real institution has done so.

Conversely, centralized assistance might improve accessibility, dispute resolution and provenance compared with costly fragmented systems. The comparison must measure **both benefits and the operator's denial power, privacy exposure, and repair burden**. A system cannot claim greater freedom merely because it offers more buttons or a larger activity catalog.

A hypothetical centralized service, an independently interoperable service and an existing less-integrated baseline are *comparators*, not descriptions of any already operational ICM system.

## 3. Minimum operational measurement, without a misleading single score
Define a preregistered set K of distinct functional goals: access to basic work, evidence retrieval, correction, lawful refusal, independent review, exit with data portability, and recovery. For actor i and each goal k, record z(i,k,M) as 1=achievable under fixed resource/time conditions, 0=not achievable, or **UNKNOWN=not observed**. Multiple user-interface labels for the same functional outcome count as one goal.

Report a **non-compensatory vector**, not a scalar claim of "freedom":

- Goal coverage and percentage of UNKNOWN outcomes (with denominators and decision rules).
- Effective refusal/exit and portability, independent evidence access and appeal latency.
- Reversible versus irreversible harm; recovery time and unrecovered burden.
- Actual operator veto capacity, false denial rate and excluded nonparticipants.
- Data collected, persons exposed, access events and disclosure/retention risks.
- Researcher/participant time, monetary costs and corrective labor.
- Independent rechecks and substantive corrections, including adverse results.

**Representation-invariance test:** splitting one meaningful action into 100 buttons must not increase measured freedom. **Missingness test:** unobserved parties or paths must remain UNKNOWN, never counted as satisfied. **Protected-floor gate:** total efficiency does not excuse a known severe violation suffered by one party.

## 4. Smallest discriminating test, not a new invented experiment result
Compare at least three predeclared arrangements on the same bounded tasks, resources and independent observations: A) baseline with locally controlled records; B) integrated support with a single authorization chokepoint; C) integrated support with independently usable alternatives and review/exit. Compare equal-cost variants and a case in which the central operator is unavailable or denies service. Also test false-positive evidence claims, omitted contributions, privacy-cost exposure and correction delay.

**Acceptance is conditional:** the new arrangement must reduce genuine verification or redress burden relative to the appropriate simpler baseline **without** unacceptable privacy, coercion, loss of independent refusal, hidden labor transfer, or severe irreversible harm. If it fails these comparisons, narrow/reject the candidate. Independent verification and participant consent must be defined before real-world deployment. No live monitoring, biometrics, penalties or private recordings are authorized by this research note.

A participation/accessibility benefit alone does not establish autonomous choice; audit logs alone do not establish event truth. Authenticity of a device is not proof that its report is accurate. Consent by one individual never grants rights over other people's personal data.

## 7. Audit disposition and next falsification
**KEEP:** Original purpose and protected-party boundaries; public provenance and negative-result preservation.  
**NARROW:** "Freedom" to observable goal accessibility and a non-compensatory vector, never metaphysical or count-of-buttons proof.  
**HOLD:** Any centralized infrastructure, identity solution or contribution mechanism until privacy, independent exit, governance and real-world legal obligations are tested.  
**REJECT AS GENERAL CLAIM:** Longer horizons always imply voluntary coexistence; greater recording automatically implies truth; greater complexity automatically improves safety.  
**Next:** use existing simple benchmarks and bounded, consent-based independent tests before adding more architecture; publish failures and withdraw unearned claims.
