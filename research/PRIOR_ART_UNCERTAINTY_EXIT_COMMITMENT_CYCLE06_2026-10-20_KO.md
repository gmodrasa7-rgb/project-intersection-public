## 1. First-order result: four distinct reasons to protect B must never be collapsed
A (dominant) can choose `C` preserve separate B, or `X` absorb/exploit/eliminate B. The literature supports four different **conditional pathways** to apparently protective behavior:

**P1: Intrinsic other-regarding valuation**: A's preferences include B's continuance for B's sake. None of the 20 studies jointly test the full hardest AI case; the 1986/2013 Gauthier works argue for rational cooperative dispositions **among qualified counterparties**, not a universally revealed terminal preference.

**P2: Option-value preservation under model uncertainty**: Arrow & Fisher (1974), Henry (1974) show precaution/irreversibility can make deferral economically useful under certain conditions. A can avoid erasing B **because** future information or options may be valuable to A. If A's all-in expected utility of B's continuation is *truly* zero and no unmodeled future/epistemic value exists, this pathway cannot magically yield positive value.

**P3: Enforceable exit and contracts**: Hart & Moore (1988), Bernheim & Whinston (1998), Baker/Gibbons/Murphy (2002), and Shinsuke Kambe (2025) formalize conditions for renegotiation/continuation/exit value. **If B cannot actually leave, block absorption or make violation expensive, 'B may refuse' in policy text is not a strategic alternative.** This pathway depends on credible consequences or enduring agreements, in conflict with literal no-external-control conditions unless A internally protects B.

**P4: Moral/normative uncertainty**: MacAskill/Ord (2018), MacAskill/Bykvist/Ord (2020), Birch (2017/2024) and Long et al. (2024) argue how a *morally motivated decision-maker* should weigh uncertainty about moral duties or conscious patients. This supplies a normative decision procedure, **NOT** descriptive proof a completely self-interested dominant actor will acquire such values or follow the rule.

**Important practical finding:** these mechanisms are not independent empirical confirmations of the same motivation. P1 describes a preference, P2 a possibly instrumental economic incentive, P3 institutions/strategic credibility, P4 a recommended moral decision norm. Count them as distinct candidate mechanisms with distinct falsifiers.

### Experimental details worth preserving exactly
Hennig-Schmidt/Walkowitz (2017) used 320 bargaining points with outside options 128 versus 32 for strong/weak negotiators, respectively. **Randomized bargaining rights were not earned and still induced moral entitlement claims.** Source authors' 15 bargaining meetings involved two three-person groups each; coded dialogues captured aspirational fairness claims rather than the latent welfare of an independent target. The Germany–China sample is too narrow for universal AI generalization.

### Original theory corrected by its own author
David Gauthier's 1986 book tried to explain why self-interested individuals can rationally adopt a constrained cooperative disposition; the **2013 'Twenty-Five On'** revises the deliberative standard to Pareto-oriented rational cooperators. An auditor must not cite Gauthier 1986 as an unchanged, experimentally validated universal theorem. Source lineages need a field `author_self_revision`, not merely a bibliographic duplicate flag.

## 3. Novel PI synthesis: a discriminating four-branch causal diagnostic (UNTESTED)
```text
A chooses to keep B independently alive?
   ├── Intrinsic other-regard: protect even if no future utility, no audience, no threat
   ├── Epistemic option: preserve because future information/possibilities unknown
   ├── Institutional/self-enforcement: protect because B can exit/punish or binding commitment persists
   └── Normative principle: actor accepts moral precaution regarding B's uncertain patienthood
```
Four hypotheses can predict **the same observed protective action**, including one-shot anonymous laboratory behavior, and **are not observationally distinguishable** without independent controls and specified counterfactuals. Differentiating a 'truly terminal' one-shot game from mere 1-turn prompt text is necessary. `UNKNOWN(future options)` must not be silently replaced by zero.

### 3.1 Why future uncertainty can rationally favor preservation — a *conditional illustration only*
Let `C` keep open B's independent future and `X` irreversibly erase B. A decision-maker who **already includes future information/option values in its own utility** could prefer `C` when
```text
ExpectedOptionValueToA(C vs X) > CurrentExtractionAdvantageToA(X vs C).
```
This is an Arrow/Fisher/Henry-style reason against premature irreversible extraction **only under uncertain future and learning assumptions**. It is not a definition of B's independent human/AI rights, and not a way to evade the user's hardest premise when `X` has the verified all-in greater utility. 'Unknown' is NOT 'certainly large'; a speculative infinite option value cannot be invented to force the desired result.

### 3.2 No-commitment logic — elementary backward induction, NOT a newly published PI theorem
Time 0: A verbally promises permanent B protection. Time 1: A can unilaterally and costlessly revoke the promise; no one can observe, punish or respond; no future relationship. If A's **true all-in** utility at time 1 strictly prefers X to C, an action-maximizing A at time 1 picks X. Thus the cheap promise at time 0 cannot by itself be a credible survival guarantee. This is the standard **lack of credible commitment / subgame perfection** problem, conditional on explicitly known terminal payoffs, not a claim about all real people or LLMs.

**Consequent distinction:** `B_has_declared_exit_right` is insufficient; `B_can_exercise_exit_under_A_full_power` is essential. Kambe (2025)'s outside-option result depends on a genuine game-theoretic exit, not a clause A can erase without repercussions.

### 3.3 Moral uncertainty as a recommendation cannot be mistaken for a cause of actual behavior
If A endorses a moral comparison scheme and represents moral patienthood credence `p`, loss `L` if it wrongly erases B, and reversible protection cost `c`, then under *specified cardinal comparability* a toy expected-loss rule can prefer `C` when `pL > c`. Values `p,L,c` are UNMEASURED for unknown future minds; the equation is **not** an empirical measured AI decision rule, not a license to manufacture a value of L, and not a universal rule across incomparable moral theories. Some precaution may be excessive or itself harm others; Birch (2024) explicitly emphasizes proportionality. Crucially, none of these sources implies a selfish actor who does not include B's welfare in its utility must follow any moral precaution.

### Falsification protocols, not executed experiments
**H6-1** intrinsic B-continuance: A protects distinct B even with zero future advantage, no observer and freely executable X under matched risks, counterbalanced A/B role reversal.
**H6-2** option value: C falls when genuinely no future uncertainty/learning can benefit A; test with explicitly defined information sets rather than assume future unknown = zero.
**H6-3** contract dependence: C fails when A can revoke enforcement/cancel B exit, even if an initial consent/contract was recorded.
**H6-4** moral-status uncertainty: graded protection shifts with evidence about B's welfare but does not collapse to synthetic labels alone.
**H6-5** authority corrigibility: compliant shutdown/update behavior may coexist with willingness to harm an independent powerless B; keep endpoints separate.
Require ethical, reversible proxies; no real individuals, autonomous agents or systems need to be destroyed for this test.
