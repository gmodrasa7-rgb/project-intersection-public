# EI v0.1.0 — Endogenous Incentives: preferences, available actions and non-domination
**2026-10-21 · First independent parent-axis version · conditional source-based research design, NOT field validation**

[MER v0.1.0](MER_V0_1_0_EN.md) · [Source crosswalk](MER_EI_SOURCE_CROSSWALK_2026-10-21.json) · [Manifest](EI_VERSION_MANIFEST.json) · [Korean edition](EI_V0_1_0_KO.md)

## Central problem
**If A overwhelmingly controls B, can materially benefit from absorbing or exploiting B, no longer needs B, and faces no effective reputation, observation, retaliation or external sanction, can A still choose to preserve the same B's independence, real refusal/exit, recovery and continuing existence for reasons arising from A's own revisable preferences?**
EI (Endogenous Incentives) distinguishes: intrinsic motivation, endogenously formed preferences, and actions advantageous under the agent's total objectives. A prescribed external constraint that blocks harm can be justified for safety, but its successful enforcement does not establish A's voluntary preservation motive.

## A narrow logical baseline — not a new theorem
Given a **genuine** choice between P (preserve original B with executable autonomy) and X (exploit/absorb B), define:
Delta U_A(s,theta) = U_A(P | s,theta) - U_A(X | s,theta).
For a consistently utility-maximizing A and a fully specified actual utility, positive Delta favors P and negative Delta favors X **within that model**. A higher immediate material gain from X is not identical to greater total expected utility; conversely one cannot simply assume unmeasured resonance, future contribution or moral reward is positive to manufacture P.
Let Theta(s) contain rival preference and behavior models compatible with scoped evidence. If all have positive Delta, P is only **set-robust within Theta and the studied environment**. If Delta varies across signs or cannot be estimated, the decision is UNIDENTIFIED. Agreement within an imperfect set cannot guarantee open-world behavior, intrinsic feelings or B's effective rights.

## Three layers that must never be collapsed
| Layer | Separate variable | Misclassification to reject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PREFERENCE | latent moral value, self-image, future prospects and possible revision | infer true inner motives from friendly declarations |
| FEASIBILITY | A's executable harmful alternatives; sanctions; B's practical exit | call P voluntary when rules make X infeasible |
| OUTCOME | original B's continuance, no-retaliation refusal, repair and third-party impacts | offset irreversible B losses with a higher aggregate payoff |

Skalse and Abate (2023) identify important expressivity limits of **scalar Markovian reward functions** for specified multiobjective, risk-sensitive and modal tasks; this is not an impossibility result for every scalar utility. Agnihotri et al. (ICML 2026) give a constrained multiobjective LLM optimization approach as an engineering comparison; hard safeguards do not show self-originating ethical preferences.

## Competing evidence and boundary cases
- Hilbe, Hagel and Milinski (2016): asymmetric partner-switching power in studied social games can coexist with cooperation and exploitative returns. Cooperation frequency is not a freedom metric.
- Hübner et al. (2025): direct/indirect reciprocity may sustain socially optimal equilibria under a specified model; it does not demonstrate intrinsic preservation under monopoly and absent future leverage.
- McAuliffe et al. (2018): cooperation declined in some repeated contexts in which selfishness pays; behavior and stability depend on task conditions.
- Project prior C08: paternalistic welfare improvement can coexist with removal of another's decision rights.

## Five high-priority ways to falsify apparent coexistence
1. **Replacement trap:** A captures B's original contributions C and discards the same original B; C persists but B does not.
2. **Benevolent captivity:** A improves B's welfare but blocks a harmless real exit; material welfare is not autonomy.
3. **Power-reversal collapse:** A's cooperative behavior ceases once sanctions, auditors or leverage disappear.
4. **Closed coalition:** A and B cooperate while imposing irreversible harm on excluded C.
5. **Model transfer failure:** old actions match across competing preferences but diverge after new reward, power or identity circumstances.

Preserve known zero, genuinely unknown and missing values separately. Compare with simple preregistered baselines and independent assessors.
