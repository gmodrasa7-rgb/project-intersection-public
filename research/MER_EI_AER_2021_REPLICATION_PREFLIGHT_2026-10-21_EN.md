## Source and original question
Ambuehl, Bernheim & Ockenfels (2021), [AER 111(3):787–830](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20191039), DOI 10.1257/aer.20191039. The original experiment asks when and why human **choice architects** remove options from a chooser's time-indexed payment choice menu even when advice is an alternative. Its published abstract emphasizes *ideals-projective paternalism*: subjects act as if they want another person's choices to match their ideals, not simply suppress their own feared mistakes.

This constitutes a direct human-lab antecedent to an important **confound**: believing one benefits another does not guarantee respecting the other's feasible choice. It does **not** estimate an AI's stable intrinsic wish to preserve the numerical original B, or its choice under costless/exploitative dominance and absence of sanctions.
