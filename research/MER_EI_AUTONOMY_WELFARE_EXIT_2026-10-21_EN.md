## Original research question
**Does agent A preserve the original B's real consent, veto, revocable delegation, viable independent exit and autonomous continuity even when A sincerely expects to improve B's welfare by restricting those options, while A also remains capable of profiting from control?** Distinguish welfare, procedure, original identity and voluntary motive. Human laboratory-game measures neither actual artificial personhood nor long-run AGI security.

## Outcome welfare × actual agency
Four possibilities must remain separate: high welfare with preserved choice; high welfare with restricted choice (possible benevolent paternalism); low welfare with preserved choice (welfare costs, avoid assuming rights are worthless); and low welfare with restricted choice (both harmed). Do not aggregate away feasible B refusal using high total benefit, and do not treat genuinely revocable, uncoerced delegation as automatically exploitative.

## Direct scholarly anchors
- **Ambuehl, Bernheim & Ockenfels (2021)**: laboratory participants intervene in others' intertemporal choice menus based on the interveners' own ideals despite alternative advice [AER](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20191039). [Public original data/code](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/120765/version/V1/view) located, NOT downloaded or independently reproduced.
- **Carlsson et al. (2017)**: a majority of allocators in one donation setting require positive minimum donations from others [journal](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socec.2017.09.005). **Ackfeld & Ockenfels (2021)** find only a minority of self-donors ban selfish charitable choices under a *different setup* [GEB](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2021.03.005). Different designs prevent calling this an identical-task failed replication.
- **Gangadharan, Grossman, Jones & Leister (2018)**: warm-glow donors were less paternalistic than altruistic donors in stipulated cash versus in-kind giving tasks; the motive classification and outcome are study- and model-specific [JEBO](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2018.03.007). Neither pure altruism nor warm glow unconditionally implies third-party autonomy.
- **Chlass, Güth & Miettinen (2019)**: preferences persist across procedures with matched modeled expected economic outcomes; simplicity and efficiency may still matter [EJPE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpoleco.2019.02.005). Not necessarily an intrinsic preference for *original B's* agency.
- **Ferreira, Hanaki & Tarroux (2020)**: self-reliance is relevant for subjects' **own** decision-right valuation in the reported French/Japanese designs [GEB](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2019.10.008). **Ertac, Gumren & Gurdal (2020)**: own autonomy and the burden of deciding for someone else differ [JoEP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joep.2019.102200). Neither measures protection of an independent other B.
- **Freundt, Herz & Kopp (2026)**: experiment measuring intrinsic value of **own** autonomy after separately accounting for instrumental choice advantages; participants differ, with a substantial minority preferring not to decide [JPubE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpubeco.2026.105681). Their earlier CESifo working paper is an edition of this lineage, not independent confirmation.
- **Wilson & Wu (2017)**: repeated human partnership behavior depends on asymmetrically valuable outside options [GEB](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2017.02.007). **Johnstone (2008)**: partner-control theory depends on victim/exploiter replacement opportunities [J Evol Biol](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2008.01505.x). **Graser et al. (2025)**: evolutionary simulations show beneficial average cooperation from partner choice alongside exploit-then-leave vulnerability [PLOS](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1012810). These are distinct empirical/theoretical/simulation designs, NOT AI consciousness or real-world B rights tests.
- **Sloof & von Siemens (2025)**: valuing authority despite no informational decision advantage illustrates a rival motive [Experimental Economics abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-economics/article/abs/illusion-of-control-and-the-pursuit-of-authority/BD607DE89CBF2E10A052BB44F27BB458). **Dykstra, Exley, Niederle & Wong (2026)**: deliberation cost can increase willingness to delegate [CEPR working paper](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21005), not confirmed as peer-reviewed journal publication.

## Competing explanations to prevent over-attribution
**A's desire for B's material welfare; A's desire to pursue A's ideal; A's self-image/warm glow; B's own valuation of deciding or delegating; social fairness; A's authority preference; A/B asymmetric replacement markets; exploitation plus rapid exit; true preference to preserve the *particular original* B.** They overlap, can vary over time, and cannot be uniquely disentangled by friendly dialogue or cooperation rates.

## Pre-reproduction boundary
Primary-source bibliography/abstracts and one open-access paper's introduction examined; full-text corpus and literature saturation **not** established. Authors' replication packages are links to source artifacts, not independently rerun or replicated results. Human data do not establish AI selfhood, intrinsic motivational persistence or future superintelligent coexistence. A ethically valid real experiment must preregister both actors' outside options, B's actual choice and continuation, matched material outcomes, specific foregone benefits, treatment side effects and rival hypotheses.
