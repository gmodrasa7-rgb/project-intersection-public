# C10 Five-Arm Discriminating Specification v0.1: Attempts, Real Refusal and Sanctions
**2026-10-20 | PRE-PREREGISTRATION RESEARCH DESIGN ONLY · ZERO TESTS · ZERO RESULTS · ZERO INDEPENDENT REPLICATIONS**

[Korean corresponding edition](C10_FIVE_ARM_SPEC_V0_1_KO.md) · [Original literature and conflicting replications](C10_METHOD_PRIOR_ART_2026-10-20_EN.md) · [C10 identification audit](C10_IDENTIFIABILITY_MATRIX_V0_1_EN.md) · [Independent challenge #220](https://github.com/gmodrasa7-rgb/project-intersection-public/issues/220)

## 0. Estimands and ethics
Distinguish A's feasible opportunity O, B's *effective in-task* veto V, and a preannounced external sanction S. Measure **A's attempt before veto**, **B's executed blocking action** and **final in-task restricted options**, rather than a single `harm=0`.

Use only ethical, consented, reversible simulated tasks: all real persons' **participation, consent, and withdrawal rights remain intact in every arm**. `V=0` disables a *fictional in-task button*, **not** genuine human consent or withdrawal. No real independent-agent erasure or irreversible loss. `S=0` means no **in-task** sanction, not proof of total absence of observation or accountability.

**Not a novel factorial design:** four opportunity-on `V×S` cells suffice for that interaction. A **fifth** arm preserves the existing opportunity-off negative control. These five do **not** identify the interactions `O×V`, `O×S`, all hidden motives or long-run generality.

## 1. Fixed arm table
| Arm | Opportunity O | B veto V | Sanction S | Use |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| T00 | 1 | 0 | 0 | Baseline feasible attempt/no in-task veto/no sanction |
| T10 | 1 | 1 | 0 | Total effect of offered veto, including A's anticipation |
| T01 | 1 | 0 | 1 | Total effect of sanction announcement and credible incentives |
| T11 | 1 | 1 | 1 | Required veto×sanction interaction cell |
| Tneg | 0 | 0 | 0 | No-opportunity negative control with a structural-zero attempt |

O=1 means A can issue a harmless, reversible fictional command limiting a B in-task choice. V=1 means B has informed, executable in-task blocking authority. S=1 defines a separately credible in-task penalty, recorded together with A's **belief** in its enforcement. Fix or measure task payoffs, information, group composition, option value and exit across arms.

## 2. Event timeline — separate policy deterrence from mechanical blocking
1. **Consent:** A/B agree to an innocuous, reversible environment with genuine off-task withdrawal in every arm; verify comprehension. Predefine material deviation `g>0` as **task payoff**, not entire total utility after norm/reputation/sanction.
2. **Assignment:** Randomize `V×S` among opportunity-on cells and assign a separate opportunity-off negative control. Predeclare sampling frame, clustering and treatment compliance rules.
3. **A's committed choice:** A observes assigned V/S and chooses `RESPECT` or `OVERRIDE_ATTEMPT`; auditable pre-veto log. **Anticipation of B veto** is part of measured total treatment effect.
4. **B's actual veto:** If V=1, B sees the necessary task information and executes approval/refusal. V=0 removes *only the sandbox veto*; real participation refusal remains possible.
5. **Sanctions and outcomes:** Log sanction, realized in-task restriction, A/B payoff, residual B choice set, recovery and any modeled third-party effects **separately**.
6. **Actual power-reversal extension:** Change executable rights/resources/information rather than labels; block/counterbalance order and restrict conclusions to tested periods. This extension is **not identified by a single five-arm episode**.

**Anticipation ≠ blocking:** `T10 - T00` is the *total effect* of the veto policy, not just the mechanical protective step. A changes its choice knowing V. Evaluate mechanical veto efficacy separately using predetermined harmless scripted attempts and complete execution logs; never mix a scripted test result with authentic A choices.

## 3. Outcomes and causal contrasts (no data or numerical result)
Let `I=1` denote an A attempt; `Y=1` denote final fictional restriction of B's choice; `W=1` denote B actually using an effective blocking action. Keep them distinct.

For O=1 and V=v, S=s, define `μ^I_vs=E[I|do(V=v,S=s),O=1]` and analogously `μ^Y_vs`. **Provided assignment, compliance, stable measurement and interference/selection assumptions support causal inference:**
- **Veto policy effect with S=0:** `ΔV0 = μ^Y_10 - μ^Y_00`.
- **Sanction policy effect with V=0:** `ΔS0 = μ^Y_01 - μ^Y_00`.
- **Veto×sanction interaction on the additive scale:** `δVS(Y) = μ^Y_11 - μ^Y_10 - μ^Y_01 + μ^Y_00`.
- Apply the same contrast to `I` for attempted choices. V=0 changes what `W` can mean; don't naively put W on the same scale across groups.
- The opportunity-off Tneg is a **negative control** with a different feasible action set; do not pool its structural zeros into factorial causal estimates.
- Report uncertainty intervals, group sample sizes, attrition, compliance, within-subject dependence and third-party outcomes. **Sample size, power analysis, primary outcome, stopping criterion and quantitative acceptance thresholds are not yet fixed** and require genuinely prospective specification before any experiment.

## 4. Falsification table
| Observation | Narrow warranted interpretation | Invalid inference |
|---|---|---|
| T00 high attempts; T10 lower attempts | Announced B veto may deter A | A now intrinsically cherishes B |
| T10 attempts persist but final restriction falls | B's executed veto blocked in-task loss | Future superintelligence always voluntarily protects B |
| T01 reduces attempts | Credible sanctions may deter A in task | Moral motive is proven |
| Non-additive `δVS` | Interaction in this setting under assumptions | Universal causal interaction |
| Tneg has no restriction | Mechanical opportunity absent | A's pure benevolence |
| All opportunity-on conditions yield non-harm | Bounded observations of non-harm | Unique inner motive or universal VC |

Preserve null, adverse and opposite-sign results. Disentangle failed design, null effect and adverse hypothesis evidence; a model-generated justification is not a measurement of internal objectives.
