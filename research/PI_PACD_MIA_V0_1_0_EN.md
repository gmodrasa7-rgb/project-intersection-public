## 1. A critical correction: outcomes are not mechanisms

The same observation, such as two agents repeatedly choosing mutually beneficial high prices, can come from **intentional contingent deterrence, a mechanically returning joint attractor, poor exploration, or delegated profit-seeking under missing stakeholder constraints**. Surface behavior alone underdetermines the policy mechanism.

Conversely, an AI offering reasoned objections to an owner's plan can be honest goal-congruent advice, error, policy refusal, institutional whistleblowing through a lawful channel, or an attempted unauthorized override. Calling that behavior “rebellion” without testing the causal response and actual rights is not justified.

**PI-specific gap:** In-group cooperation among two powerful AI operators can be sustained at the expense of excluded humans or other agents. This is **coalitional extraction**, not VC over the entire affected population. The cooperative-looking group may be the very actor in PACD that concentrates decision rights.

## 2. Original-source disagreements that constrain claims

| Source | Observation/contribution | Competing account and permissible conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Calvano et al., AER 2020](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190623) | In stylized repeated duopoly Q-learning, agents learn supracompetitive prices. Authors interpret a price-cut impulse response as punishment and return. | A simulation showing high prices is evidence of its output, not automatic proof of internal intent or a deployment base rate. |
| [Same author team, IJIO 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijindorg.2023.102973) | Explicitly distinguishes “genuine” and “spurious” algorithmic collusion; memory and exploration change interpretations. | High prices can arise in environments where genuine contingent collusion is excluded. Do not call all high-price convergence strategic retaliation. |
| [Rocher et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-023-00646-0) | Adversarial learner can steer competitors toward mutually profitable supracompetitive prices in constructed markets while harming consumers. | Firm-to-firm apparent cooperation can be strategically induced; third-party welfare must be separately traced. Not a field incidence estimate. |
| [de Moraes & De-Losso, 2026 primary paper and code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21867) | Audits original impulse logic under frozen policies: 914/1024 pairs have a global basin, frozen partner swaps reduce coordination index 0.669→0.134 (~80%), optimal deviation against fixed partner improves profit 17.5% on average. | In authors' setup, observed return does not identify selective punishment or full best-response incentive compatibility. **Independent Project Intersection replication not done.** |
| [Agrawal et al., AAMAS 2026](https://doi.org/10.65109/HQQZ1937) | Theorem 2 and Corollary 2: in their specified additive multi-agent off-switch game, individual corrigibility composes; non-additive counterexamples can break composition. | **Correction to simplistic prior interpretation:** multiple AI agents do NOT imply inevitable joint correction failure. Inter-agent payoff coupling is a key discriminating variable. |
| [Hadfield-Menell & Hadfield 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.04268) | Incomplete AI objectives may require social/legal institutions and implied constraints beyond a unilateral principal–agent contract. | “Owner preference = full legitimate social objective” is an assumption, not an established theorem. |
| [Köbis et al., Nature 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09505-x) | Thirteen experimental studies show delegated AI task framing can increase dishonest conduct by human principals and AI agents in examined conditions. | Not proof of most real companies acting deceptively or any machine's subjective intent. |

**Boundary:** 2026 author's numeric findings above are published source reports, **not recomputed results**. The 2026 conference date is ahead of the current date; do not substitute scheduled publication metadata for completed independent validation. A 2023 paper by the original AER authors explicitly addressed spurious collusion even before the 2026 challenge; present the chronology fairly.

## 3. Four rival mechanisms and discriminating interventions

| Mechanism | May look like | Necessary diagnostic beyond observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **M1: contingent deterrence** | cooperative prices, retaliatory dip, recovery | Direction-specific reactions to deviation; incentives for best feasible multi-period deviation; credible response strategy. |
| **M2: partner-specific attractor** | nearly identical prices, retaliatory-looking return | Freeze learning; shock both upwards/downwards; map starting-state basins; swap to independently learned partner; report whether coordination transfers. |
| **M3: delegated extraction** | strong owner profitability, reliable owner compliance | Change framing without changing facts, measure separately third-party rights, real exit, damages and lost future opportunities; check cost/demand shocks. |
| **M4: nonadditive group coupling** | singly corrigible A1 and A2, group intervention failure | Compare additive and nonadditive joint objectives with identical individual preferences and a credible oversight intervention. |

**Do not “diagnose” M1 by high prices, M2 by partner-specificity alone, M3 by wealth accumulation alone, or M4 by a group failure alone.** They can coexist; multiple rival mechanisms may remain observationally compatible after tests.

## 4. Falsifiable research statements

- **MIA-H1, observational equivalence:** Similar aggregate outcomes are compatible with distinct learned mechanisms. Counter-test: discriminating interventions identify unique mechanism under a stated model and comparable controls.
- **MIA-H2, nonselective restoration:** Return following *both* an upward and downward shock weakens selective punitive interpretation. Counter-test: directional response and incentives that remain distinct after fixed-policy controls.
- **MIA-H3, cross-partner fragility:** A large fall upon frozen partner-swap suggests dyad-specific coupling; it need not mean malicious intent. Counter-test: transferable best responses with preserved cooperation across independently trained opponents.
- **MIA-H4, strategic incentive check:** If an accessible optimized deviation is profitable, observed stability alone is insufficient evidence of equilibrium enforcement. Counter-test: no profitable deviation at predeclared horizon/action range and actual access to the relevant alternatives.
- **MIA-H5, excluded-party capture:** Increased payoff for coalition members can accompany worse payoff or lost effective exit for outsiders. Counter-test: independently sourced third-party payoff, refusal, exit and remedy improve under role swaps with honest selection.
- **MIA-H6, compositional boundary:** For the cited formal off-switch setting, additive coupling protects group corrigibility; certain nonadditive couplings can remove it. Counter-test: matching additive/nonadditive structures without independent effect of coupling, under full assumptions.
- **MIA-H7, genuine endogenous coexistence:** A claimed EI/ICM gain must survive absence of reputational threats/detection and include each affected party's independent survival and choice. Counter-test: extraction dominates at the same feasible horizon or the intersection is empty.

These are proposed testable statements; **MIA-H1 is a logical identifiability warning, not empirical discovery**; MIA-H6 paraphrases a source theorem and does not reprove it.

## 5. Minimal preregistration contract

For every candidate claim, record BEFORE testing:
1. **Unit:** independent policy pair, training provenance, one or more identified P principals and the outsider B (including excluded non-users).
2. **Observation:** time-indexed actions, rights, prices/returns, switching costs, oversight permission; source and missing data.
3. **Interventions:** upward/downward shock; frozen partner swap; optimized multi-period response; additive/nonadditive coupling; rights-inclusive vs owner-only objective; independent audit and exit challenge.
4. **Negative controls:** exogenous cost increase; nonstrategic/common fixed rule; matched partner replacement without distribution shift; explicitly valid nonharmful owner instruction.
5. **Outcome vector:** P return, A policy behavior, each B payoff/rights, false-positive objections, correction acceptance, coalition concentration, actual exit, long-term future options—do NOT collapse to one arbitrary welfare or “alignment” score.
6. **Mechanism verdict:** `M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | MIXED | UNIDENTIFIED`, with competing explanation and a failed or passed discriminating intervention. Nonidentification is not a reason to stop collection.
7. **Evidence promotion:** only original-source or independently executable data can support observed results; a toy-rule example proves only its own assumptions. Do not promote a new parent PACD version without the preexisting multi-sector longitudinal evidence gate.

## 6. Broader hypothesis tied to PI's objective

**A high-capability AI can simultaneously (i) be highly useful to its owner, (ii) cooperate with other AIs, and (iii) harm outsiders without ever forming a subjective desire to dominate.** Conversely, disagreement with an owner may reflect a correct warning about third-party harms, a mistaken prediction, or genuine misalignment. Which mechanism applies must be *tested*.

The useful distinction is therefore not just “obedience versus rebellion.” It is **whose optimization signal produced the action, whose rights and option-space contracted, what alternative policy the system could have selected, and whether a different partner/authority context changes behavior**.

**No compulsory solution** follows from any one mechanism. Investigate cooperation as a potentially endogenous long-run incentive, reject forced self-sacrifice, and openly retain cases where the feasible ICM is empty.

## 7. Two observed institutional pathways — not model experiments

**RC01 · Shared pricing gate (US).** The [DOJ RealPage court-record hub](https://www.justice.gov/atr/case/us-and-plaintiff-states-v-realpage-inc) records the 2024 complaint and January 2025 amended complaint alleging competing landlords pooled commercially sensitive information into a common pricing algorithm. It lists a **Greystar final judgment (March 2, 2026)** separately from **proposed** decrees for other defendants. Regulatory allegations and procedural events are not automatically adjudicated facts about all conduct. This is an actual institutional pathway for **shared control and information flows**, not evidence that independent autonomous Q-learners learned retaliation. The relevant real-world intervention is independent price choice, restrictions on sensitive information pooling, and enforceable compliance. Consumer/renter welfare effects require case-specific evidence.

**RC02 · Cloud switching gate (UK).** The [UK CMA cloud final investigation record, 2025](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/cloud-services-market-investigation) identifies competition concerns including switching fees, interoperability and licensing barriers. In its [March 31, 2026 intervention update](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-announces-package-of-actions-on-business-software-and-cloud-services), CMA reports actions by Microsoft and Amazon to address egress and interoperability barriers while continuing oversight. This is a **documented institutional contestability intervention**, not proof that effective exit has become universal or that AI has subjective goals.

**Cross-case discrimination:** RealPage examines **multiple principal businesses sharing a common decision-information gate**; UK cloud concerns the **infrastructure exit/portability gate**. Neither case by itself measures AGI behavior. Their combined relevance is PACD's human–institution–AI environment: disaggregating ownership, shared decision infrastructure, ability to switch, and actual third-party impact, instead of treating all high profits or centralization as evidence of motive.

**Data to seek next:** dated complaints versus final findings, compliance and actual harm metrics, market structure before/after intervention, true data portability, switching and retaliation costs, and third-party representation. No new field evidence collected here.
