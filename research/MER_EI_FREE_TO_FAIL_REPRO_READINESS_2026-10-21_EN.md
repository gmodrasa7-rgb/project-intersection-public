## Distinct original paper, not replication of Ambuehl et al. 2021
Bartling, Cappelen, Hermes, Skivenes & Tungodden, *Free to Fail? Paternalistic Preferences in the United States* — [original 2023 IZA Discussion Paper 16151](https://docs.iza.org/dp16151.pdf), 59 PDF pages. The original authors' [ifo listing](https://www.ifo.de/en/publications/2026/contribution-refereed-journal/free-fail-paternalistic-preferences-united-states) calls The Economic Journal version **forthcoming** at this audit. 2023 numerical tables must NOT be represented as independently confirmed unchanged figures from the eventual 2026 journal manuscript.

Two human US general-population spectator experiments. The first randomizes whether helping a mistaken stakeholder entails **hard option removal** (forced safe payment) or **soft truthful information** (maintain the risky option) and manipulates whether the information error arose internally or externally. The original 2023 manuscript reports the following Study1 take-up percentages:

| Error attribution | Hard, options removed | Soft, information only |
|---|---:|---:|
| Internal | 33.5% | 85.9% |
| External | 30.0% | 87.5% |

The paper reports **55.0 percentage points** as the pooled randomized *intervention-modality effect on acceptance* (p<0.01). These are ORIGINAL-AUTHOR FIGURES independently read in the publicly available 2023 PDF, **not participant-data reanalysis**. Study1 n=8,004; Study2 n=6,033. Study2 theorized participant types (roughly half welfarist, one-third libertarian-paternalist) are **model-based descriptions**, not mind measurements.

## Research limits
The causal result concerns *which type of offer spectators accept in that experiment*, not A's intrinsic care for the continuing identity of a particular B, profits from dominating it, independent B veto/exit, or power reversal. An informational soft intervention may retain nominal menu choice while still not proving informed consent or practical independence. Behavior does not identify exclusive motives (warm glow, welfare, self-image, norm following and future option value remain rivals).
