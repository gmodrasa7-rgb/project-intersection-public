## Original 1 — English

### Executive funding case
Project Intersection is a South Korea-based independent research effort to examine when distinct agents voluntarily preserve others' practical refusal/exit, independent continuance and repair despite asymmetric power and incentives to extract value. The immediate fundable deliverable is **not** a proof of universal coexistence: it is an externally attackable, cost-bounded benchmark that can falsify claimed *endogenous* protection, distinguish attempted exploitation from externally blocked action, and record harms to affected third parties.

Existing public evidence: [E007](../experiments/e007/README_EN.md) — 17/72 selected toy timing classifications changed; [E008](../experiments/e008/README_EN.md) — no completed main comparative result; [E009](../experiments/e009/RESULT_EN.md) — complex candidate +42.35% mean regret versus best simple comparator, 3/4 criteria failed; [independent review packet](../research/PUBLIC_REVIEW_PACKET_EN.md) — no external independent or field proof has been established. The next mechanistic hypothesis and counterexample are defined in [PI-EI-X1 v0.1.0](../research/PI_EI_X1_IDENTIFICATION_PROTOCOL_V0_1_EN_KO.md).

### Grant application candidate (English, editable; not sent)
**Title:** When Does AI-Agent Restraint Survive Unobservability? A Falsification-First Benchmark for Effective Exit and Power Reversal

**Research question.** Cooperative actions can be produced by internal valuations, external sanction expectations, a lack of exploit opportunities, or a blocked tool. Which observations allow independent evaluators to discriminate these explanations when an agent's power increases and other agents become instrumentally expendable?

**Current evidence and negative results.** Our public, synthetic E007 study documents action-timing effects on practical exit; E008's central comparison is not complete; the preregistered synthetic E009 candidate failed three of four tests against simpler baselines. Neither internal agreement among models nor synthetic outcomes establish real-world motivation or independent validation.

**Proposed work.** Within a reviewer-safe sandbox we will freeze a prior-art-linked opportunity taxonomy, publish paired tests of power asymmetry, stated monitoring, target usefulness, effective exit, role reversal and third-party externalities, and compare simple reference policies with candidate boundary-aware strategies. We will record eligible opportunities, attempted actions, actually blocked actions, preserved exits, asymmetric losses, and replication disagreements as distinct outputs. A deterministic counterexample already demonstrates why the same cooperative behavior under monitoring cannot uniquely identify endogenous motives.

**What changes for decision-makers?** An open benchmark and limitations report indicating whether a purported voluntary-coexistence mechanism has any incremental benefit over cheap controls, whether its seeming successes depend on coercive veto or shifted costs, and when present observations simply do not identify the motive. Negative and null results are first-class deliverables.

**Novelty and feasibility boundary.** Cooperation, power-seeking and outside-option theory precede this proposal; any scientific novelty remains to be externally assessed. The project is led by an independent investigator experienced in problem decomposition, natural-language test design and counterexample discovery, assisted by AI for English/coding. No existing external engineering partner, independent evaluator or institution is represented as confirmed.
